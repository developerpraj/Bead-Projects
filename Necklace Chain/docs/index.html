What is Necklace Chain?

For everyone

Rival fans usually only meet on the pitch. Necklace Chain gives them a reason to meet in the middle. It is inspired by the Ekecheiria, the ancient Olympic truce, and runs as a game inside Telegram on the Base network.

The game in four moves

  1. Pick a side and stake. For a match (the pilot is the unbranded "North Red vs North White" derby), you stake $BEAD on the Red or the White side of the Truce Pool. Your wallet is locked to that side for that match.
  2. The match is played. Staking closes at kickoff. The result is read from a sports-data feed and published on-chain, then there is a 30-minute window in which anyone qualified can challenge a wrong result.
  3. The truce test. If both sides took part in a balanced way (the smaller side holds at least 30% of the pool), the truce holds and every holder gets their stake back plus an equal share of a bonus pool funded in advance. If the pool was lopsided, everyone simply gets their stake back (1:1). If the match is cancelled, everyone is refunded.
  4. Forge a Cosmic Necklace (optional). Collect 54 tokens of each side (trading with a rival fan) and burn them to forge a one-of-a-kind NFT, the Rama Node. You give up that match's bonus in exchange for a lasting badge that grows in rank the longer you keep it.

Why it exists

Balance is rewardedThe bonus only pays when both sides are represented, so nobody profits from a one-sided pile-on.

Common questions

Is this an investment?

No. $BEAD is a game token. There is no yield for holding it, no pre-sale and no promise of profit. The truce bonus is a game reward paid from a pool funded in advance for a specific match. You can lose value (for example through the 1% tax or market moves) and nothing here is financial advice.

Can I get my stake back?

Before kickoff you can withdraw what you staked (the 1% deposit tax is not returned). After a result, you claim your staked amount back one-for-one even if the truce fails. If a match is cancelled or the data feed goes silent for 48 hours, everyone is refunded their staked amount. The 1% deposit tax is never returned, even on a refund. Claims never expire.

Who decides the result?

A Chainlink network reads the result from a sports-data API and reports it on-chain. UMA's optimistic oracle then holds it for 30 minutes so a wrong result can be challenged. Anyone who has staked at least 33 $BEAD (a "Chotki") can challenge by posting a bond; a frivolous challenge loses that bond.

Are the contracts safe?

They are heavily tested (129 automated tests, including randomised and invariant testing) and the first release is a deliberately small "guarded pilot": one match, with a hard cap on how much can be staked. They have not had a professional audit, so treat it as experimental.

Is it connected to a real club or league?

No. The pilot uses culturally inspired, unbranded names. There is no affiliation with any club, league or federation.

Do I need crypto or ETH?

You need $BEAD to stake. You do not need ETH: network fees for the supported actions are sponsored by the project's paymaster (within daily limits).

Whitepaper (as built)

Concise protocol description, matching the code in this repository

1. Abstract

Necklace Chain is a Base-chain protocol that turns sports rivalries into a cooperative staking game. Opposing fans stake the same token into a per-match Truce Pool. A symmetry rule rewards balanced participation. Participants can burn equal amounts of both sides' receipt tokens to forge a non-financial NFT credential. A fixed 1% Peace Tax on transfers is forwarded, in the same transaction and without custody, to a public-goods recipient.

4. The Truce Pool

Each match has one pool with a state machine: OPEN → LOCKED → PENDING_RESOLUTION → SETTLED or REFUNDED, with a DISPUTED sub-state. Depositing mints a 1:1 receipt token for your side (after the 1% tax). Rules that protect participants:

  • One side per wallet per match (an affiliation lock), so a single wallet cannot hold both sides to forge for free.
  • Symmetry floor: the bonus pays only if the smaller side holds at least 30% of the pool.
  • 50-block hold on deposits before they can be claimed, blunting flash-loan and last-second tactics.
  • Pool cap: a fixed maximum amount of $BEAD per match bounds worst-case exposure in the pilot.
  • Fail-safes: refund if no oracle result arrives within 48 hours of kickoff; a disputed match that is not resolved within 7 days is refunded.

5. Cosmic Necklace (Rama Node NFT)

Burning 54 Home and 54 Away receipt tokens (and the matching 108 $BEAD of principal) mints an ERC-721. It carries no monetary yield. Its utility is non-financial and read on-chain: voting weight (1× to 6× as tenure grows in 108-day cycles), a fee-rebate tier (0% / 15% / 30%), and a cosmetic rank. Any transfer resets the tenure.

6. Governance and staking tiers

TierStakeRole
The Chai18 $BEADParticipation and polls
The Chotki33 $BEADMay challenge an oracle result by posting a bond; the stake is slashed to the public-goods forwarder if the challenge is rejected
The Master Council99 $BEAD, 99 seatsOff-chain votes (Snapshot) on match whitelisting, dispute escalation and an emergency circuit breaker. No control over any funds.

Passed votes are executed on-chain by a multisig (Gnosis Safe) acting as the Council Executor. There is no on-chain governor in V1.

7. Oracle and disputes

Results come from a Chainlink Runtime Environment (CRE) workflow that reads a sports API and reaches consensus across nodes, then delivers a signed report on-chain. The report opens a UMA Optimistic Oracle V3 assertion with a 30-minute challenge window. Undisputed assertions finalise automatically; disputed ones follow UMA's resolution and the pool's refund rules.

8. Treasury and public good

The Peace Tax is forwarded inside the same transaction by an immutable pass-through contract that never holds a balance. The recipient is fixed at deployment.

9. Principal risks

  • Smart-contract risk: unaudited code. Mitigated by tests, a small pilot and a hard pool cap, not eliminated.
  • Oracle and data-source risk: a wrong result is possible; the challenge window and refund paths limit but do not remove this.
  • Regulatory risk: the design avoids passive yield and pre-sales, but classification of tokens varies by jurisdiction. Legal review is pending.
  • Market and liquidity risk: a new token can be illiquid and volatile.
  • Dependency risk: Base, Chainlink CRE, UMA, the Coinbase Smart Wallet and Telegram.

How it works

A match from start to finish

Where the result comes from

1 RequestOwner or keeper calls requestMatchResult; an event is emitted
→
2 CRE workflowNodes read the sports API and must agree on one outcome
→
3 ForwarderA signed report is delivered to MatchResultReceiver
→
4 UMAResolver opens a bonded assertion, 30-minute window
→
5 SettleAnyone calls finalizeSettlement; holders claim

Outcome codes: 1 home win, 2 away win, 3 draw, 4 cancelled/refund. Unfinished matches report nothing and are retried.

A worked example (from the rehearsal)

A Red fan and a White fan each deposit 1,000 $BEAD. The 1% tax leaves 990 each. The match is drawn, the truce holds (50/50 ≥ 30%), and a 1,000 $BEAD bonus pool is split equally: each fan claims 990 + 500 = 1,490 $BEAD. If one side had been under 30% of the pool, each would just claim their 990 back.

Gasless play

The Telegram mini-app signs a UserOperation with the player's passkey wallet and attaches the project paymaster. The paymaster only sponsors a short allow-list of calls (approve, deposit, withdraw early, claim, forge, stake, unstake) with per-action and daily spending limits, so it cannot be drained by arbitrary transactions.

report = abi.encode(uint64 chainSelector, uint256 requestId, uint256 matchId, uint8 outcome)

Workflow rules (cre/resolve-match/outcome.ts): FT/AET/PEN use teams.home/away.winner (both null = draw); PST/CANC/ABD/AWD/WO = cancelled; every other status throws so nothing is reported early; non-empty errors, empty responses and a fixture-id mismatch also throw. Fixture ids must be numeric.

Security model and trust assumptions

  • Reports are accepted only from the configured forwarder, for an open request, on the expected chain selector, once; with identity enforcement, only from the expected workflow id and author.
  • Receiver owner can change forwarder and identity settings: hand it to the Safe (TRANSFER_RECEIVER_OWNERSHIP=true) and use a separate requester key for routine requests.
  • Paymaster spends only on allow-listed calls within caps; it needs ETH deposit plus a locked stake at the EntryPoint.
  • Webapp: validated paymaster endpoint, security headers allowing Telegram framing only; keep real keys out of NEXT_PUBLIC_* variables.
  • Known limits: no external audit; CRE workflow has not run in the real runtime; API key is a normal secret (visible to node operators).

Gotchas

  • No git in this project by design. Libraries were installed with forge install --no-git.
  • Foundry vm.prank/expectRevert hit the next call (cache getters first); keep vm.setEnv scenarios in one test.
  • Do not leave deploy environment variables in a shell; they change the deploy-script test. verify-all.ps1 clears them.
  • Behind a TLS-inspecting proxy set NODE_OPTIONS=--use-system-ca; never disable strict-ssl.

Honest assessment: strengths, risks, costs and how to mitigate them

Internal view for the team and engineers. Remove this section before sharing the page publicly.

Bottom line: a good, unusually well-built experiment, but an unproven product. The pilot is cheap enough to find out. The real spend is on legal, liquidity and operations, not code. This is a candid opinion, not legal or financial advice; cost figures are rough ballparks, not quotes.

What is strong

  • An original, easy-to-explain mechanic: rival fans share a pool and balance is rewarded.
  • Regulatory thinking built in: no passive yield, no pre-sale, no treasury discretion, an NFT with non-financial utility only.
  • As read from the contracts, payouts do not depend on who wins, only on whether the match was played and the pool was balanced. That is far less like betting than typical sports tokens (legal must still confirm).
  • Solid pilot engineering: 129 tests, pool cap, refund fail-safes, rehearsal against the real UMA oracle.

Can it succeed?

  • As a technical showcase or learning pilot: already a success.
  • As a real product with lasting users: the odds are low, as with most token games, because demand is untested rather than because the code is weak.

Whale pads, reward 500Yes-0.51%Pays out 500-8 BEAD (-0.04%)

What it shows. This is not theft: the whale pays the 1% tax, locks its capital through the match, and the honest fans actually end up better off (+1.95% instead of -1.00%). But it is bonus farming: the whale took about 31% of the reward as bonus (about 26% after its tax) for one cheap deposit, because the 1% tax is the only cost of participating and the 30% cliff makes padding cheap. Padding pays whenever the weaker side's bonus rate is above roughly 1%. Break-even is about reward > 0.02 × padding / whale share of the side, which here is about 526 BEAD.

Mitigations to evaluate (each is a design choice; any contract change needs new tests and legal review):

  • Size the reward to the pool. A reward below about 0.5-1% of the pool removes the incentive, but also most of the appeal.
  • Smooth the cliff so padding earns in proportion instead of unlocking a lump sum.
  • Per-wallet caps on stake or bonus. Weak alone because wallets are cheap and fees are sponsored; pair with wallet verification or reputation if you want them to bite.
  • Early-deposit weighting so last-minute padding earns less than early participation.
  • Source the bonus from sponsors rather than the token allocation, so a padded truce costs the project less.
  • Monitor deposit concentration per side and alert the team when one wallet holds most of the minority side.

What the three cases say together

  • The contracts behaved as designed in all three cases: every claim executed without a revert and the payouts matched the rules. This is a behaviour check of a few crowds, not a proof of correctness.
  • Fans earn a bonus of about 2-5% of their stake per match when the truce holds and lose 1% when it fails. That is real money per match, so the reward size is the main economic dial, and a legal question (could it be seen as yield?).
  • The weak points are product and incentive design (the cliff, farming, who funds the bonus), not smart-contract bugs.

Findings and conclusion

Internal view. Opinion based on the code, the tests and the simulations above; not legal or financial advice.

Findings

  1. The mechanics work as coded. 129 tests pass and three simulated match nights ran end to end on the real contracts with correct taxes, bonuses, refunds and burns.
  2. The winner of the match does not change who gets paid. Settlement treats a home win, an away win and a draw the same way; the result only decides "played" versus "cancelled" and when claims open. The sports result is a calendar and a story, not a bet. That makes the oracle stack and sports-data licensing heavy for what they decide.
  3. The bonus is a subsidy, not income. In the simulations it is worth roughly 2-5% of stake per match, paid from a reward pool that someone funded. In the token model the funder pays in the project's own token, which is circular.
  4. The 30% line is a cliff. At 29% nobody gets a bonus; at 30% the bonus switches on. That invites last-minute padding of the weak side (Case 3) and means a lopsided crowd simply loses the 1% tax for nothing (Case 2).
  5. Steering works. The smaller side earns more per BEAD (5.05% versus 2.16% at the 30% line), which nudges fans toward balance as intended.
  6. The 1% tax is never returned, even on a refund, and it goes entirely to Protocol Guild. Good for the story and for trust; it also means usage earns the team nothing.
  7. Weak points are product and incentive design, not code. No simulated case revealed a funds-loss bug, but this is a behaviour check of a few crowds, not an audit.

Conclusion

Necklace Chain is technically sound and unusually well tested for a pilot, but as currently framed it has no outside payer. Fans want belonging and status. Sponsors, leagues and public-good funders want positive engagement. The token only moves value between the participants themselves, so the rewards can only be paid by early token holders or by the project. That is the real problem: not "can we build it" (we can) but "who benefits and who pays".

The strongest parts of the work are reusable infrastructure: a balanced-pool mechanism, a Chainlink CRE to UMA settlement pipeline with refunds and fail-safes, gasless onboarding in Telegram, immutable public-good forwarding, and a non-financial tenure credential. The weakest part is the token-and-bonus framing, which carries the circular economics and the legal question about yield.

DimensionVerdictWhy
TechnologyStrongTested, simulated, rehearsed against the real oracle contract
DemandUnprovenNo real fans have used it

Recommendation in one line: keep the engine, change the business model. Reposition from a token game to a sponsor-funded fan-unity platform with the settlement infrastructure as a reusable asset, and make $BEAD optional until demand is proven.

What the research says

  • The proven demand is for objective-trigger payouts (insurance and relief), where an outside party has a real reason to pay and a payout rule that nobody argues about. That is the problem our oracle and refund machinery already solves.
  • Matching mechanisms are established but need identity checks. Quadratic funding exists because broad support is worth more than big cheques, and it has to defend against fake participants. Our whale simulation found the same weakness.
  • Outcome betting draws gambling regulators. Polymarket, the best-known prediction market, trades mostly sports markets and, per Wikipedia, has been blocked or banned in many jurisdictions, and Minnesota enacted a law banning prediction markets from 1 August 2026, which the US Department of Justice moved to challenge. Necklace Chain pays the same regardless of who wins; keep it that way, and ask counsel about any change that ties payouts to results.
  • Chat-app reach is large but borrowed. Telegram's audience is a strength and a dependency. Keep the contracts and wallet independent of any one platform.

Suggested order, and the most promising real-world fit

  1. Sponsor-funded unity campaign with a sports-for-peace non-profit (original purpose, no token, lowest regulatory weight). Approach organisations like PeacePlayers International and local fan groups after checking their current programmes.
  2. Charity matching variant using the forwarder and a verified recipient, once identity checks are chosen.
  3. Parametric community cover through an existing provider (Etherisc- or Arbol-type partners): we supply the settlement, challenge and refund layer and the Telegram onboarding; the partner supplies licence, pricing and capital. This is the largest real problem and the biggest build.
  4. Open-source resolution toolkit in parallel as a low-cost hedge.
